The story

A window for the agents you already run.

I built Shika to stop juggling worktrees and terminals when several coding agents are in flight. I use it every day, including to build Shika.

Why The name What it stays Using Shika

Why

Each task needed its own checkout. I wanted one place that showed who was working, who was asking, and who was ready, without reading every terminal to find out.

Shika is that window. It starts the CLI you already pay for, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor CLI, or Pi, in a real terminal, with your login and your plan. Shika doesn't wrap a model API. Your provider, your settings, and your bill stay where they were.

The name

Shikamaru is the one who keeps track of everyone on the field while you give directions. Shika is also the Japanese word for deer, which is the mark on the app.

The cards are that field. You write the prompt in the terminal. The window keeps score, and a notification names the task that needs you.

What it stays

You keep your git. Shika makes the worktree and the branch. Create PR commits only after you confirm the preview. Close can discard or push, and it never commits on its own. A push you type in the shell leaves the card up until you close it.

You keep your machine. There is no Shika account and no telemetry. The CLI talks to its provider. Git talks to your remotes. Create PR uses your GitHub CLI. Worktree setup runs only after you approve the commands in the repo.

You keep one task on screen. The others keep running. The CLI's own terminal is the conversation. Shika keeps the cards, the worktree, and a shell beside it.

It is a Mac app, and it is early. When something new needs explaining, it gets a section in the guide. If a step you needed is missing, open an issue.
